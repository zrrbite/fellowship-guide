# The Weekly Session Plan

Written for four people who play together **one night a week** and want to keep progressing without
homework between sessions.

## The structural advantage we already have

**Matchmaker's Boon** rewards stable parties with **+25% gold and materials** and **increased drop
chances on gems and crafting materials**. It is earned by repeatedly completing dungeons with the
same group.

A fixed weekly four-stack qualifies for this permanently. Groups that pug do not. This single fact
means our effective farming rate is materially higher than a solo player putting in the same hours,
and it is the strongest argument for never filling a missing slot with a stranger. **If someone
cannot make it, consider rescheduling rather than pugging.**

Season 3 also added **Discord integration for party invites** and **Communities** (global chat
groups) — worth setting up once so the weekly assembly costs zero minutes.

## Session template — roughly three hours

| Block | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| **0. Assemble** | 5 min | Everyone respecs to tonight's build. Nobody is doing this mid-pull. |
| **1. Warm-up Adventure** | 15 min | One easy dungeon at a difficulty you have already cleared. Shakes off the week. Do not push here. |
| **2. The farm block** | 60-75 min | 4-5 Adventure runs of **Scryer's Peak** and **Ruins of Regath**. This is the Seal of the Heskyr farm and the bulk of the night's value. |
| **3. The push** | 45-60 min | One league push, or a Capstone attempt. **Time-box it.** If it is not going well after three attempts, abandon and return to block 2. |
| **4. Weekly objectives** | 20 min | The Pinnacle Dungeon (Xul) if unlocked — first clear each week grants **5x rewards**. Bloodstones are once weekly per difficulty. |
| **5. Housekeeping** | 10 min | Upgrade gear with gold, hand in Artisan Supply Boxes, socket the gems you picked up. |

**The rule that keeps sessions fun:** block 3 is the only place wipes are allowed. Blocks 1, 2 and 4
should be routine. A group that spends the whole night wiping on a push has made no progress and had
no fun; a group that farms two dungeons and then pushes once makes progress either way.

## Where our time actually goes

**Adventures** are 10-15 minutes with one boss. **Capstones** are 28-32 minutes with three bosses.
For a group with a fixed budget, Adventures are almost always the better rate — three Adventures
beat one Capstone for loot per hour, and a failed Capstone costs half an hour with nothing to show.

Run Capstones when you need them: they gate league progression, and they are the source of several
set bonuses.

## What to farm, and why

**Priority one: Scryer's Peak and Ruins of Regath.** These two Season 3 Adventures are the only
Adventure-tier source of **Seal of the Heskyr** (+25% Gem Power), which is BiS for every hero in the
game. They are also rated easy. Nothing else in the game gives a casual group this much value per
minute.

**Priority two: Empyrean Sands, Sailor's Abyss, Silken Hollow.** The next-safest Adventures, and
between them they cover Haunting Lament (healers), Sinthara's Veil (tanks), Death's Grasp (DPS) and
Sin Warding. Full mapping in [dungeons.md](dungeons.md).

**Avoid until overgeared:** Urrak Markets and Everdawn Grove. Both punish positioning mistakes with
full wipes. There is no reward there worth the frustration on a Tuesday night.

## Realistic season goals

Do not aim for Eternal 60. For a once-a-week group, a good season looks like:

| Milestone | Roughly when | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Clear the Contender capstone | Week 1-2 | Unlocks Adept and real loot |
| Reach ~180 ilvl, clear Adept | Week 3-4 | Dungeon curses begin; this is the first real skill gate |
| Reach Champion, unlock weapon traits | Week 5-7 | **King of the Hill** on every non-tank. Biggest survivability jump in the game. |
| Two sets equipped, Heskyr among them | Week 6-9 | Your gems suddenly do 25% more |
| First Legendary | Week 8-12 | Builds branch here; respec to the variant on your hero page |
| Comfortable in early Eternal | End of season | A genuinely good outcome for four people playing once a week |

The Pinnacle Dungeon (Xul) unlocks at **Eternal 5**. Treat it as a stretch goal, not a plan.

## Standing role assignments

Fixed roles are worth more to a casual group than optimal ones. Muscle memory is the scarce
resource, not theorycraft.

**Tank** owns: route decisions, pull size, facing dangerous mobs away from the group, and calling
the stop. The tank is the pace-setter — if the tank is uncertain, the pull is too big.

**Healer** owns: dispels, and saying "no" to the next pull. A healer who says "let me drink" is
saving the group four minutes of corpse running.

**DPS** own: interrupts. This is the single biggest difference between a casual group that clears
and one that does not. **Assign interrupt targets before the pull, not during it.** See
[interrupts.md](interrupts.md).

**Everyone** owns: soaking. A surprising number of Fellowship mechanics are "someone must stand in
the circle", and they wipe groups purely through nobody claiming it.

## Between sessions — the five-minute version

Nobody should have homework. But if someone has five idle minutes:

- Spend gold on upgrades. Gold is the least precious resource and upgrading is pure ilvl.
- Run Quickplay for Supplies, Marks of Fellowship and Artisan Supply Box rotations.
- Socket any gems sitting in the bag. Unsocketed gems do nothing.

Do **not** burn crafting materials on gear below your league's exit item level. It is the most
common way casual players waste a season's materials.

## Bringing an alt

If someone wants a second hero: farm the **135 ilvl token set** via Quickplay, then run Cithrel's
Fall and Heart of Tuzari to catch up. **Master Craftsman Braggi** converts account-wide resources
into small gems, so gem progression is not restarted from zero.
